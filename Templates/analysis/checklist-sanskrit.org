* Padapāṭha and sandhi
1. The padapāṭha gives every word in its pausa form: /vigatāḥ malāḥ/,
   not /vigatā malā/; /kalayet/, not /kalayec/. The sandhi form belongs
   only in the recitation line.
2. Describe only junctions that actually occur in the transmitted text.
   Never invent a contraction the text does not have (e.g. /malāvacchedakā/,
   /tāsāmeva/). Never claim "no sandhi change" where the text shows one
   (/ityakāla°/ = /iti a°/; /yāsāv/ = /yā asau/; /yadvakṣyati/ = /yat
   vakṣyati/).
3. Standard rules to apply correctly: final /-aḥ/ before a voiced sound
   → /-o/; /-āḥ/ before a voiced sound → /-ā/; /-t/ before a voiced sound
   → /-d/; /-t/ + /c/ → /-c c/; /i/ + a dissimilar vowel → /y/; /au/ + a
   vowel → /āv/. Where the text keeps a hiatus (e.g. a quoted lemma before
   /iti/), say "left without sandhi". Do not treat it as a contraction.
4. Do not justify sandhi with Pāṇini sūtras unless the sūtra is the right
   one. A plain description is better than a wrong citation.

* Roots, stems, derivation
5. /jananī/, /janaka/: √jan causative ("beget, generate"). Not √jan (1)
   "be born".
6. Do not derive nouns from pseudo-roots. /mala/ is a noun ("impurity"),
   not √mal; /saṃskāra/ and /nyakkāra/ come from √kṛ (8), not √skār or
   √kār; /hṛdaya/ is the stem "heart", not /hṛd/ + /-aya/; /rūpiṇī/ is
   /rūpa/ + /-in/, not √rūp.
7. Abstracts in /-ya/ take vṛddhi of the base: /svātantrya/ ← /svatantra/,
   /vailakṣaṇya/ ← /vilakṣaṇa/, /prādhānya/ ← /pradhāna/, /āñjasa/ ←
   /añjas/. Do not split them into roots and invented suffixes (/-trya/,
   /-ṇya/, /-nya/).
8. Write suffixes in their base form: /-ana/, not /-aṇa/ (the ṇ comes from
   retroflexion after r/ṣ); ppp. /-ta/, not /-dha/; future /-sya-/, not
   /-ṣyati/.
9. Give the verb class correctly and consistently. /vidyamāna/ is the
   present participle of √vid (4) "exist". It is not a ppp.
10. Distinguish the participles and verbal adjectives: ppp. (/-ta/, /-na/),
    present participle (/-at/, /-māna/, /-āna/), gerundive (/-ya/, /-tavya/,
    /-anīya/). /vācya/ is a gerundive. It is not a ppp.

* Forms and agreement
11. Check gender and number by agreement. An adjective takes the gender of
    its noun (/mala/ is m., so /vigatāḥ … avacchedakāḥ/ are m. nom. pl.). An
    attribute of /svātantrya/ is n. Do not assign a neuter where the n.
    pl. would be /-āni/.
12. A bahuvrīhi in /-āśrayā/ etc. agreeing with a feminine noun is f. nom.
    sg. Do not call a nominative an "instrumental for metrical reasons".
13. A word quoted from the root text (pratīka) before /iti/ is given in the
    form in which it is quoted (often the stem). It is the subject of
    /uktam/, /ucyate/ etc. These passives are not "impersonal".
14. /yat/ introducing a clause is normally the relative pronoun (n. nom./acc.
    sg.) with a function in its clause, e.g. object of /vakṣyati/. Do not
    call it an "adverbial quasi-conjunction" without reason.
15. Locative + /api/ without a participle is concessive ("even if there is
    …"). It is not a locative absolute.

* Compounds
16. Before resolving a compound of the root text, look for the commentator's
    own vigraha in the same or the following sentences, and follow it.
    Examples: /tac ca ubhayam/ is a karmadhāraya (not "tayoḥ ubhayam");
    /kulaṃ śarīraṃ yasya tat/ is a bahuvrīhi; /tataḥ sphuritabhāvaḥ/ is a
    pañcamī tatpuruṣa.
17. A possessive in /-in/ (/rūpiṇī/, /apekṣin/) is a taddhita formation, not
    a bahuvrīhi. /ananyāpekṣin/ is a nañ-tatpuruṣa of an /-in/ formation.
18. A compound "X-ādi" means "X etc." (bahuvrīhi on /ādi/, m. i-stem). Keep
    the junction visible: /avidyā-vāsanā-ādi/, not /°vāsanā-dīnām/.
19. Do not resolve a compound by repeating a member of it ("advayanayasya
    nayaḥ"). Do not label one compound with two alternative types ("ṣaṣṭhī
    or karmadhāraya") without saying which one the analysis follows.
20. Spelling: bahuvrīhi, not bahuvīhi.

* Reading the commentary
21. In Jayaratha's viveka, a demonstrative (/tat/, /asau/) refers to what
    the commentary is explaining at that point. Determine the referent from
    the preceding gloss and say what it is in brackets: "[the pair]", "[the
    Heart]". Do not refer it to the whole compound of the verse by default.
22. If the commentary breaks off a quotation, say where it breaks off. Do
    not fill in the rest without marking it.
23. Do not correct the transmitted text silently. A reading different from
    KSTS goes into the notes with its source.
