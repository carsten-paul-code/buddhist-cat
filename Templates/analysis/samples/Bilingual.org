*Translation:* Why are there just five aggregates (skandha)? — On account of their bringing to light (udbhāvanatā) the basis of self (ātmavastu) in five modes: the self-basis that is the body together with its possessions (saparigraha-deha), the self-basis that is experience (upabhoga), the self-basis that is conventional expression (vyavahāra), the self-basis that is the forming of merit and demerit (dharmādharmābhisaṃskāra), and the self-basis that is the support of these (tadāśraya).

*Sandhi:* pañca + ākāra → pañcākāra (a + ā → ā) · ākāra + ātma → ākārātma (a + ā → ā) · vastu + udbhāvanatām → vastūdbhāvanatām (u + u → ū)

*Forms:*
- /pañca-ākāra-ātma-vastu-udbhāvanatām/ — udbhāvanatā- f. (ā-stem): abstract in -tā from udbhāvana- n., action noun in -ana of the causative of ud-√bhū (1, bhvādi, P: bhavati; caus. bhāvayati „make appear“); dvitīyā vibhakti ekavacana (acc. sg.); object of /upādāya/
- /upādāya/ — lyabanta (gerund) of upa-ā-√dā (3, juhotyādi, U: dadāti); „having taken“, used as a postposition with the accusative: „on account of“

*Compounds:* /pañca-ākāra-ātma-vastu-udbhāvanatām/ — ṣaṣṭhī tatpuruṣa: pañcākārasya ātmavastuna udbhāvanatā „the bringing to light of the five-mode self-basis“; within it ātma-vastu ṣaṣṭhī tatpuruṣa (ātmano vastu „the basis/thing of the self“) and pañca-ākāra bahuvrīhi qualifying it (pañca ākārā yasya „having five modes“)

*Structure:* /…-udbhāvanatām/ [object, acc.] /upādāya/ [postposition: „on account of“] → the answer, headed by the general statement; 24–28 list the five modes

*Note:* ātmavastu is the thing taken as self, not the self: the skandhas bring to light five bases on which the idea of a self rests.

*Concepts:* *ātmavastu* — the „basis of self“: what the notion of a self is projected onto; the AS derives the number of the skandhas from the five ways such a basis appears.

⟦23⟧ *Translation:* On account of their bringing to light (udbhāvanatā) the basis of self (ātmavastu) in five modes: \\
*Rahula/Boin-Webb 2001:* Because of the five methods by means of which *[the idea] of self (ātman)* makes its appearance: \\
*DharmaMitra:* Because they serve to manifest the objective basis of the self (ātmavastu) in five ways: \\
→ Rahula/Boin-Webb 2001: ātmavastu as „[the idea] of self“ — the basis (vastu) is dropped

* Sentence
** Grammar
One question and its answer, the catechism form of the AS.  The question (22) is a nominal sentence with an understood copula: kasmāt pañcaiva skandhāḥ.  The answer (23–28) has no finite verb: six accusatives in -udbhāvanatām, each governed by the gerund upādāya used as a postposition („on account of“).  23 states the principle — the self-basis appears in five modes —, 24–28 list the five, each a compound ending in -ātmavastūdbhāvanatām.

** Tibetan rendering
The Tibetan keeps the structure: /zhe na/ marks the question, /phyir te/ opens and /phyir ro/ closes the answer, /dang/ joins the five modes.  It renders the key terms consistently — /ātmavastu/ as /bdag gi gzhi/ („basis of self“), /udbhāvanatām upādāya/ as /brjod pa'i phyir/ („because of stating“).  That last choice shifts the sense: in the Sanskrit the skandhas bring the self-basis to light (/udbhāvanā/, from the causative of ud-√bhū); in the Tibetan they state or express it (/brjod pa/) — the translators read /udbhāvana/ in its sense „making known, declaring“.

** Translations compared
*Translation:* Why are there just five aggregates (skandha)? — On account of their bringing to light (udbhāvanatā) the basis of self (ātmavastu) in five modes: the self-basis that is the body together with its possessions (saparigraha-deha), the self-basis that is experience (upabhoga), the self-basis that is conventional expression (vyavahāra), the self-basis that is the forming of merit and demerit (dharmādharmābhisaṃskāra), and the self-basis that is the support of these (tadāśraya).

*Rahula/Boin-Webb 2001:* Why are there only five aggregates? Because of the five methods by means of which *[the idea] of self (ātman)* makes its appearance: self as *physical apprehension*, self as experience, self as expression, self as *the agent* of all right and all wrong, and self as the basis of all that.

*DharmaMitra:* Why are there exactly five aggregates (skandhas)?
Because they serve to manifest the objective basis of the self (ātmavastu) in five ways:
1. By manifesting the objective basis of the self as the body together with its possessions;
2. By manifesting the objective basis of the self as the object of experience;
3. By manifesting the objective basis of the self as the ground for conventional designation (vyavahāra);
4. By manifesting the objective basis of the self as the formative activity (abhisaṃskāra) of merit and demerit (dharma-adharma);
5. By manifesting the objective basis of the self as the support (āśraya) for those [preceding aggregates].

Rahula/Boin-Webb reduce ātmavastu to „self“ and turn the forming of merit and demerit into „the agent“; DharmaMitra keeps the basis („objective basis“, with an added „objective“) and stays close to udbhāvanatā („manifest“).  Neither reflects the Tibetan's „stating“ (brjod pa).
