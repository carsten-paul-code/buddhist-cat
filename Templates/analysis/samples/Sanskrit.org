*Sandhi:* madvāg aśaktā: final -k voiced before a vowel → -g · aśaktā + api → aśaktāpi (ā + a → ā) · yat + niyantraṇam → yan niyantraṇam (t before a nasal → n) · niyantraṇam ullaset: m before a vowel stays · ud + laset → ullaset (d + l → ll)

*Forms:*

- /aśaktā/ — aśakta- adj. (a-stem, f. -ā); negated bhūte kṛdanta (ppp., kta) of √śak (5, svādi, P: śaknoti „he is able“); prathamā ekavacana f.; predicate of /mad-vāk/

- /ullaset/ — ud-√las (1, bhvādi, P: lasati); vidhiliṅ (optative), parasmaipada, prathamapuruṣa ekavacana (3rd sg.); present stem lasa- + -īt → laset

*Compounds:* /mad-vāk/ — ṣaṣṭhī tatpuruṣa: mama vāk „my speech“ (mad- = asmad- in composition)

*Structure:* atra [adverb] · /mad-vāk/ [subject] /aśaktā/ [predicate] /api/ [concessive] → concessive clause · /yat niyantraṇam/ [subject] /ullaset/ [V opt.] → relative clause, taken up by /tat/ in 686

*Note:* madvāgaśaktāpi hides the privative a-: mad-vāk aśaktā api „though my speech is incapable“ — not mat-vāk-śaktā „capable“; the a- disappears in ā + a → ā. The optative in a generalising relative clause: „whatever … may“.

*Concepts:* *niyantraṇa* — control, regulation; here the ordering of the words of the commentary, which Jayaratha does not claim as his own achievement.

* Sentence
** Grammar
Two clauses. The first (685) joins a concessive clause — atra mad-vāk aśaktā api, „though my speech is incapable here“ — to a relative clause, yat niyantraṇam ullaset, whose optative generalises („whatever control may shine forth“). The second (686) is the main clause: the correlative tat takes up yat … niyantraṇam and receives two nominal predicates, pārameśvaram and the compound śrīmat-mahā-ānanda-vijṛmbhitam; the copula is understood. The verse is the commentator's humility formula: any mastery in what follows is the Lord's, not his.

** Translations compared
*Translation:* Although my speech (vāc) is incapable (aśakta) here, whatever control (niyantraṇa) may shine forth (ullaset) — that belongs to the Supreme Lord (pārameśvara): it is the unfolding (vijṛmbhita) of glorious great bliss (mahānanda).

*Dyczkowski 2023:* Although my speech is powerless here (in this task), whatever *restriction* may arise (on one's understanding of the teachings is not my fault, rather) that is (all) the venerable expansion of the Supreme Lord's great bliss (which both hides and reveals the truth). (7)

*DharmaMitra:* In this matter, whatever *unfettered expression* may emerge, even though my own voice is powerless, that is the unfolding of the glorious Great Bliss of the Supreme Lord.

All three read the concessive clause alike. They part over niyantraṇa: Dyczkowski takes it as a restriction on the reader's understanding (and adds that it is not the author's fault), DharmaMitra reverses it into „unfettered expression“; the translation here reads control or ordering of the words, which the Lord, not the commentator, brings about.
